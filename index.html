Hello

I am a PhD candidate in Mechanical Engineering at the University of Toronto, working in the areas of robotics and autonomous systems. My work sits at the intersection of control theory, state estimation, and multi-robot coordination.

My background spans aerospace engineering and mechanical engineering, with degrees from universities in Iran and Canada. Over the years my research has moved from spacecraft attitude control to robotic swarms, but the thread running through all of it has been the same: how do you design systems that can reliably make decisions and act in uncertain environments?

I am originally from Iran and currently based in Toronto, Canada. Outside of research I try to keep a consistent morning routine, stay active, and build small useful things with code.

Research

My PhD research focuses on multi-robot search and multi-target tracking in GPS-denied environments. In plain terms: how can a group of robots work together, without any central controller, to find and track multiple moving targets when they cannot rely on GPS?

This involves developing algorithms for decentralized coordination, probabilistic decision-making, and cooperative sensing through wireless sensor networks. The goal is systems that are scalable, robust, and practical for real-world deployment.

Before this I spent several years working on spacecraft attitude control, including contributing to the Iris CubeSat, which launched in June 2023. That work gave me a strong foundation in Kalman filtering, state estimation, and control systems design that continues to shape how I approach problems today.

I have published in the Journal of Intelligent and Robotic Systems and IEEE Sensors Journal, and presented at international conferences in Canada and South Africa. I hold a Canada Graduate Scholarship from NSERC and have received fellowships from the University of Toronto, the Canadian Space Agency, and Google.

You can find my publications on Google Scholar.

Projects

Outside of my main research I enjoy building small coding projects, mostly in Python. I find it a good way to stay sharp, explore ideas, and occasionally solve a real problem in my own life.

One recent example is a Morning Ritual Gate, a macOS app that blocks your computer until you have meditated and written 10 things you are grateful for. It came out of a genuine need for structure during PhD life, where nobody sets your schedule for you and it is easy to let good habits slip.

I have also worked through machine learning and AI courses, completing nanodegrees from Udacity in partnership with AWS and Intel. Those projects are available on my GitHub.

I enjoy the process of taking something from an idea to a working thing, whether it is an algorithm, a simulation, or a small tool that makes daily life a little better.
